## Summary

This release corrects the executable nine-lever crosswalk, rebuilds every score from one canonical component pipeline, and regenerates the complete print, web, map, linked-data, and open-code atlas. The corrected model identifies greater capital exclusion: the bounded global estimate rises from `$15.87T` to `$19.29T` annually.

## Seven Signals and the Double-Counting Question

The prior component table represented nine conceptual levers but populated lever 6 with lever 3's `v2clkill` value and lever 7 with lever 4's `v2xcl_acjst` value. That duplication did not cause the software to add national capital exclusions twice. The old nine-term score can be written exactly as seven unique signals with the two paired signals retaining their original two-ninths weights:

`R = (L1 + L2 + 2L3 + 2L4 + L5 + L8 + L9) / 9`

Across all 193 countries, this weighted seven-signal expression produces exactly the same scores and downstream figures as the former nine-row representation; the maximum score difference is `0.0`. This equivalence does not apply to an unweighted seven-signal average, which would change the model's weights.

The concern therefore exposed a crosswalk-transparency and proxy-selection problem, not an arithmetic duplication of the capital total. Correcting the crosswalk preserves the intended nine-lever model while giving every conceptual lever a distinct operational signal:

- Lever 3: `v2clkill`, freedom from political killings.
- Lever 4: `v2xcl_acjst`, access to justice index.
- Lever 6: `v2x_clphy`, physical violence index.
- Lever 7: `v2clrspct`, rigorous and impartial public administration.

With the proper fields, levers 3 and 6 differ in 60 countries, and levers 4 and 7 differ in 95. Sixty-five national RTLP scores change. The corrected population-weighted regression yields `eta = 0.356`, operationally rounded to `0.36` under the existing 25% contextual cap.
